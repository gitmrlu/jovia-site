Notice of Privacy Practices

Last updated: October 10, 2026

Jovia is not live yet. Jovia is not yet providing care, and no medical or health information is collected on this website. This notice describes how protected health information will be handled once care is offered through the Jovia platform. It does not cover joviahealth.com or the waitlist; those are covered by our Privacy Policy.

THIS NOTICE DESCRIBES HOW MEDICAL INFORMATION ABOUT YOU MAY BE USED AND DISCLOSED AND HOW YOU CAN GET ACCESS TO THIS INFORMATION. PLEASE REVIEW IT CAREFULLY.

Who this notice applies to

When Jovia launches, clinical care offered through the Jovia platform will be provided by independent, licensed health care providers (“Providers”) through affiliated, physician-owned medical practices (each, a “Practice,” and together with Jovia acting on a Practice’s behalf, “we” or “us”). The Practice is a “covered entity” under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (“HIPAA”). Jovia provides technology and administrative services to the Practice as its “business associate” and is contractually required to protect your information in the same way.

“Protected health information” (“PHI”) means information that identifies you and relates to your past, present or future physical or mental health or condition, the care provided to you, or payment for that care, such as your medical history, skin photos, diagnoses and prescriptions.

Our responsibilities

  • We are required by law to maintain the privacy and security of your PHI.
  • We will let you know promptly if a breach occurs that may have compromised the privacy or security of your PHI.
  • We must follow the duties and privacy practices described in this notice and give you a copy of it.
  • We will not use or share your PHI other than as described here unless you tell us we can in writing. If you give us permission, you may change your mind at any time by letting us know in writing.

How we may use and disclose your PHI

For treatment

We use and share your PHI to provide and coordinate your care. For example, your Provider reviews your intake answers and photos to evaluate your skin, writes a prescription, and sends it to a pharmacy to be filled and shipped to you. We may also share information with other clinicians involved in your care.

For payment

We use and share PHI to bill and get paid for the services you receive, for example by processing your payment for a visit or for medication. Jovia does not currently bill health insurance.

For health care operations

We use and share PHI to run the Practice, improve care and contact you when necessary. For example, we may review records to evaluate quality, train clinical staff, or send you reminders about check-ins and refills.

With business associates

We share PHI with companies that perform services for us, such as Jovia, pharmacies, and providers of secure hosting, messaging and payment processing. They must sign agreements requiring them to protect your information and use it only as we permit.

Other uses and disclosures permitted or required by law

We may also use or share your PHI without your authorization in these situations, only as permitted by law:

  • As required by law, including to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services to show we are complying with federal privacy law;
  • For public health and safety, such as reporting adverse reactions to medications, preventing disease, helping with product recalls, reporting suspected abuse, neglect or domestic violence, or preventing a serious threat to anyone’s health or safety;
  • For health oversight activities authorized by law, such as audits, investigations and licensure;
  • In lawsuits and legal actions, in response to a court or administrative order or a valid subpoena;
  • For law enforcement purposes or with a law enforcement official, as permitted by law;
  • For research, when approved through a process that protects your privacy;
  • For workers’ compensation claims, and for special government functions such as military, national security and presidential protective services;
  • With coroners, medical examiners and funeral directors, and with organ procurement organizations; and
  • To people involved in your care, such as a family member or friend you identify, unless you object.

Uses that require your written authorization

We will never sell your PHI or use or share it for marketing without your written authorization. Most uses and disclosures of psychotherapy notes also require your authorization. Some states give extra protection to certain types of information, such as mental health, substance use disorder, HIV/AIDS and genetic information, and we will follow those stricter laws where they apply.

We may de-identify your information so that it no longer identifies you, as permitted by HIPAA. De-identified information is not PHI.

Your rights

When it comes to your health information, you have the right to:

  • Get an electronic or paper copy of your medical record. Ask us for a copy of your records and other PHI we have about you. We will usually provide it within 30 days, and may charge a reasonable, cost-based fee.
  • Ask us to correct your medical record if you think it is incorrect or incomplete. We may say no, but we will tell you why in writing within 60 days.
  • Request confidential communications, such as asking us to contact you only by a certain email address or phone number. We will say yes to all reasonable requests.
  • Ask us to limit what we use or share for treatment, payment or operations. We are not required to agree, and may say no if it would affect your care. If you pay for a service out of pocket in full, you can ask us not to share that information with your health insurer, and we will agree unless a law requires us to share it.
  • Get a list of those with whom we have shared your information (an “accounting of disclosures”) for up to six years before the date you ask, other than for treatment, payment and operations and certain other disclosures. We will provide one list a year for free.
  • Get a copy of this notice at any time, including a paper copy on request.
  • Choose someone to act for you, such as a legal guardian or someone with medical power of attorney.
  • File a complaint if you feel your rights have been violated (see below).

To exercise any of these rights, contact us at privacy@joviahealth.com.

Complaints

If you believe your privacy rights have been violated, you can complain to us at privacy@joviahealth.com. You can also file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office for Civil Rights by visiting hhs.gov/ocr/complaints or calling 1-877-696-6775. We will not retaliate against you for filing a complaint.

Changes to this notice

We can change the terms of this notice, and the changes will apply to all information we have about you. The new notice will be available on request, in the Jovia platform, and on this page. The Practice that provides your care will give you its notice before your first visit.

Contact

Privacy questions or requests: privacy@joviahealth.com.
